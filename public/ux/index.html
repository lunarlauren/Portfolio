A NOTE ON AI

Curiosity first. Tools second.

I use AI the way I use a sketchbook: to get unstuck, test an idea, or see a problem from another angle. It's useful for brainstorming, exploring code, and getting a rough thought into something I can react to.

But the interesting part is still the thinking. I decide what matters, question what comes back, and shape the final experience myself. I don't want AI to replace my taste, my drawings, or the messy work of figuring something out. I want it to give me more room to do that work.

How I made this portfolio

A SMALL CASE STUDY · PERSONAL PROJECT

A portfolio that thinks out loud.

I wanted this site to feel like the work behind the work: curious, a little imperfect, and carefully made. It needed to show the finished design and leave room for the questions, sketches, and decisions that get it there.

An editorial notebook

The dotted paper, Newsreader headlines, and compact uppercase labels borrow from a working sketchbook. I paired them with DM Sans body copy so the personality stays warm while project details stay quick to scan. The portrait illustration carries that same idea from sketch to screen.

Details with a job to do

Client projects read like labeled files, each with a clear challenge, my contribution, delivery, and design approach. A square top-left corner, small folder tabs, fine rules, and restrained arrows make the archive feel tactile without competing with the case-study story. On smaller screens, the same hierarchy stacks into a straightforward reading order.

Color that changes with the light

Warm paper and charcoal ink anchor the light palette. In dark mode, paper becomes near-black (#151515) and text softens to pale gray (#EFEFEF). The LL mark keeps its navy-to-pink signature, while a clear blue action accent helps links and controls stand apart. Project colors stay with their own file tabs instead of taking over the whole page.

  • Paper · #F5F3EF
  • Ink · #171717
  • LL navy · #213544
  • LL pink · #FE81AE → #F15C9D
  • Action blue · #235F88

The goal was a site with a point of view that still gets out of the way: expressive in its details, clear in its structure, and comfortable to use in either theme.
